# Native Registry

Generated by `tools/Generate-NativeRegistry.ps1`. Do not edit by hand.

366 routines are admitted for every naturally observed call at
their address. Across those routes 3,452,755 of 3,522,701 observed
calls (98.0%) were proven native; the remainder fall back to emulated
assembly by design, because the dispatcher returns no replacement for a
state it has not been proven against.

Release state-census route (`--native-cycle-census`, measured 2026-08-13):

The resulting state census is byte-identical to the assembly-only
baseline (`9036a9de…`), so that 52.45% is faithful, not merely running.
The last three rows matter because the host credits a native call when
it *offers* one. A replacement the core then refused would inflate the
share silently, and a refusal count of zero is what rules that out.
**Nothing is withheld any more.** `02:57E9` and `03:6054` each used to
move the census on their own and were held out of every composed
deployment for it, with their own regressions asserting the moved hash.
Both turned out to be replacement faults rather than properties of the
routines, and both were the same shape: a boundary charged the wrong
number of cycles, with the error taken back off a later instruction so
the body total stayed right. A correct total is exactly why a state
census could tell that something was wrong and never where.

`03:6054` charged `ADC $26` 9 master cycles instead of 12 and gave the
three back on the taken branch at `606E`, which put both of its writes
three cycles early. One write trace scoped to the four addresses the
routine touches, diffed against assembly, localised it to two rows.
`02:57E9` charged `CMP $3980,X` 18 instead of 15 the same way, and also
published the caller's N and Z at the boundary after `LDA $2D40`
instead of the load's. That routine writes nothing at all, so no write
trace could have shown the second fault -- it is visible only as the
status an interrupt stacks when it lands on that boundary.

Both now reproduce the assembly census, call trace and write trace
exactly, and their regressions assert the baseline. The hold-back
mechanism stays in `kWithheldRoutines` in `src/app/main.cpp` for the next
routine that needs it; the list is empty and every run says so.

## Cycle budget

A whole-ROM `--instruction-profile` of the same deployed route (6,389
executed addresses, 1,292,378,139 master cycles) shows what the
remainder is:

| Category | Master cycles | Share of all cycles | Share of working cycles |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Frame-sync spin loop (`00:E2FB`–`E39C`) | 584,757,771 | 45.25% | — |
| Timer sound driver (`00:FC00`–`FE48`) | 177,034,446 | 13.70% | 25.02% |
| Transcripts resumed after its RTI | 95,769,795 | 7.41% | 13.53% |
| Music sequencer (bank `45`) | 32,952,048 | 2.55% | 4.66% |
| Video banks (`08`, `09`) | 96,288,942 | 7.45% | 13.61% |
| Gameplay pool | 305,575,137 | 23.64% | 43.18% |

Nearly half the machine is idle: `00:E323` is `CMP $00` / `BEQ -4`,
spinning on the VBlank counter 24.2 million times. That is the game
waiting, not working, so it is not a porting target and it is the slack
that absorbs anything the port makes faster.

The second row needs the third to read honestly. The profile charges each
address the span to the next instruction hook, and the driver ends in an
`RTI` that returns into whatever the timer interrupted. When that is a
suspended checkpointed transcript, the rest of it replays with no hook in
between, so those cycles land on the driver's `RTI`: `00:FC8D` shows 398
master cycles per execution against a real cost of 21. Those 95,769,795
cycles are other routines' native bodies, already counted as native, not
sound-driver work. Every other address in the driver profiles at a clean
6, 12, 15, 18 or 21.

Both the second and third rows grew when the epilogue slice landed, and
the music sequencer's row fell by about as much. Nothing moved between
banks: carrying the driver's transcripts all the way to the `RTI`
removed the epilogue's own hook samples, so a sequencer transcript that
the timer suspended now replays with no sample between the `RTI` and
itself, and its cycles land on `00:FC8D` instead of in bank `45`. The
third row is exactly the mechanism, which is why it has to be read
together with the second.

The pool is the residual, so the six rows sum to the profile total exactly.
An earlier revision quoted it 7.9M high, which made the working-cycle column
add to 101%; the rows above are recomputed and the column now adds to 100%.

## The sound driver

The timer interrupt handler is the sound driver, and it now runs
natively. It is a flat state machine over `00:FC00`–`00:FE48` with no
subroutine calls at all: three PSG channels walked at X = 4, 2 and 0,
each streaming five-bit DDA samples out of a sequence bank mapped into
MPR6 with `TAM #$40` and pushed through the PSG waveform port at
`$0806`. The eight banks it selects between are listed in ROM at
`$FC06`, immediately ahead of the handler's first instruction.

Earlier reports placed bank `45` here and called it the PSG driver. That
was wrong. `$FC06` holds `48 49 4A 4B 4C 4D 4E 4F`, so the driver's data
banks are `48`-`4F`, mapped at `$C000` and only ever read. Bank `45`
runs at `$4000`-`$4CB8` and is the music sequencer, a separate component
that programmes the PSG registers `$0801`-`$0809` directly. It has its
own section below.

Taking it needed the transaction write channel widened past work RAM and
the four VDC ports. It now also carries the PSG register file
`$0800`-`$0809`, the timer at `$0C00`-`$0C01`, and the interrupt
controller at `$1402`-`$1403`. Each is a whole register file on the
hardware page whose writes are ordinary stores with no wait state, so a
transcript prices them like any other instruction — unlike the VDC
ports, which cost three extra cycles. Device writes are invisible to
a work-RAM census, so `--bus-write-range` and `--bus-write-trace` were
added to census them directly. The state census would catch a wrong
value on its own, since it hashes the audio as well as work RAM, but
only as a changed hash somewhere in 3,600 frames. The regression
asserts all 954,241 PSG, timer and interrupt-acknowledge writes,
byte-identical to assembly.

Two things bound what the transcript may cover. From the `CLI` at
`$FC32` the handler is interruptible, and a transcript is computed once
from one snapshot — so it cannot observe a write a preempting handler
makes to memory it later reads. The epilogue reads `$DA`, the rerun
request such a handler sets, and the walk's own `STZ $DA` at `$FC30`
would make that read always zero, so the handler would never take its
`JMP $FC03` chain exit into the sequencer. The walk therefore cannot
carry one transcript across that read. It runs the epilogue as a
second transcript instead, re-entered after the body so its snapshot
contains the write — see the epilogue section below, including why the
two meet either side of the `SEI` rather than at the read itself.

The other is `CLI` itself. The core samples the interrupt on an
instruction's last cycle, and `OP_CLEARF` runs that sample before it
clears the flag, so the instruction after a `CLI` still runs masked. A
checkpoint that published the post-`CLI` status opened the window one
instruction early and a nested timer interrupt arrived nine master
cycles ahead of the machine. The `CLI` checkpoint must publish the old
mask and let the clear land on the next one.

**The driver is now admitted on every arrival: 254,990 of 254,990.**
It reached only 113,164 of them until the core learned to nest a
replacement. The rest arrived while another checkpointed transcript was
suspended, and the core would not consult the replacement hook while one
was active — so a timer interrupt taken inside any long transcript ran
the driver emulated. Suspension is machine state, not a lock: the
handler is 65C02 code like any other, and the interrupted transcript is
saved to a frame stack while the handler's own transcript runs. See
`third_party/beetle-pce-fork/patches/0007-huc6280-nested-replacement.patch`.

That rule used to make the port fight itself, and the palette slice
below is where it first showed. A long transcript shadowed the driver
for its whole body: `00:E9B6` runs 9,745 cycles, spans about two timer
periods, and the interrupts that landed inside it could not reach the
driver's hook. Making the three palette leaves native added 9,937,182
cycles and gave back 694,000 of them in driver entries. Every slice paid
that tax and the toll grew with transcript length. Nesting removed it:
the driver, the VDC handler chain and the sequencer between them
recovered 85,778,658 cycles at once, and a long transcript now costs
nothing anywhere else.

The exception was worth as much as the rule while it lasted, and the
reasoning outlives it. `00:EB30`, added in the
same area a slice later, runs up to 18,585 cycles — more than three
timer periods — and cost the driver nothing at all: not one entry, not
one cycle. Every one of its 3,599 calls arrives with `I` already set,
because the upload has to land inside the blanking interval, so no
timer interrupt was ever going to be delivered inside it. Body length
is only half the question; whether the routine runs masked is the
other half, and it is one `--observe-hook` run to find out.

The wrapper slice below then settled what the cost actually scales with,
and it is not per-call length. Its nine transcripts are 36 to 162 cycles
each — a fortieth of `00:E9B6` — and there are 52,911 of them, 4,849,479
cycles of native time in total. They cost the driver 1,326 entries and
756,342 cycles, 15.6% of the slice's own gross, which was the largest
shadowing bill any slice had paid at the time. Total
native time is the quantity that sets the scale, not per-call length: a
thousand small transcripts shadow the driver exactly as well as one large
one. The split inside the slice is the proof: its three masked wrappers run
inside the VBlank handler and cost nothing at all, and the whole bill comes
from the six that run on the main thread.

### The epilogue, and a second instruction a checkpoint cannot express

Nesting left the driver's body running natively on every arrival and
its epilogue running emulated on every arrival — ten instructions,
26.4M cycles, 2% of the machine, and by a wide margin the largest
thing the ranking instrument could still see. It is now taken, for
25,180,701 net, which moved the deployed registry from 49.97% to
51.92% and the driver itself from 145,282,473 native cycles to
170,463,174.

There are two copies of the epilogue, twelve identical bytes each,
assembled at `00:FC7C` for the channel walk and `00:FD70` for the
track-advance tail:

```
FC7C  PLA           FC84  LDA $DA     the rerun request
FC7D  TAM #$40      FC86  BEQ $FC8C
FC7F  PLY           FC88  PLA
FC80  PLX           FC89  JMP $FC03   chain into the sequencer
FC81  SEI           FC8C  PLA
FC82  STZ $D9       FC8D  RTI
```

The `LDA $DA` is why the walk had stopped here rather than run on. A
transcript is computed once, from the snapshot taken when the core
offers the replacement, and `$DA` is written by `00:E12A` inside the
video handler — which preempts this one, because `FC32`'s `CLI` opens
the window deliberately. So the value the epilogue branches on can
arrive after the snapshot that would have to predict it. That is not
hypothetical: `00:E12A` fires 387 times on this route, and `00:FC89`
and `00:FD7D` between them take the chain exit 387 times, so a
transcript deciding the branch from entry state would have taken the
wrong arm on every one of them.

The fix is to make the epilogue two transcripts instead of one, and
re-enter the walk after the body has run so the second one gets a
fresh snapshot. That is the `00:EAB0` pattern from the palette
stepper, used for a different reason: there it was length, here it is
staleness.

Where the two meet is the finding. The obvious split is at the read,
`00:FC84`, and it is wrong. A checkpoint carries one status byte, and
`ApplyReplacementCheckpoint` derives two different things from it:
`P`, which an interrupt taken at that boundary pushes and an `RTI`
later restores, and `IFlagSample`, which is what the core's timer
dispatch actually tests. For every other instruction in the ROM those
two want the same answer. `SEI` is the one that does not: it retires
with `I` set, so a correct `P` has the bit — but its `LastCycle()`
sampled the mask *before* `P |= I_FLAG`, so the boundary after it is
still open, and a correct `IFlagSample` does not have the bit.

Publishing the open mask was tried first, on the reasoning that it
mirrors the `CLI` deferral the walk already models at `FC32`. The
census caught it in frame 951: an interrupt was delivered at the `SEI`
boundary that assembly did not deliver, and the stack showed why —
the handler pushed `PC=$FC82, P=$02`, an `I` clear that its `RTI`
would have restored, silently reopening a window the driver had just
closed. Publishing the true `P` instead is not right either: it shuts
a boundary the hardware leaves open. Neither byte is the machine.

So the `SEI` stays emulated and the two transcripts meet on either
side of it — the first stops in front of it, the second is entered at
`00:FC82` and `00:FD76`, the instruction after. It costs 1,526,046
cycles of the 26.7M the epilogue was worth, 5.7%, and buys an exact
answer for the other 94.3%.

This is the second instruction the checkpoint format cannot express,
and the pair is worth reading together. `BBR` at `00:F423` could not
be expressed because of *when* the core steps — two clock steps where
the machine takes four, which moved a video event. `SEI` cannot be
expressed because of *what* a checkpoint carries — one status byte
where the machine distinguishes two. Both were found by the census
rather than by reading the core, both cost a low single-digit
percentage of their slice, and in both cases the fix was to hand the
instruction back rather than to widen the format. The format is
scaffolding that phase 4 deletes; an exception carved into it would
outlive its usefulness.

Verification is the two-run diff against pure assembly: the state
census, 1,604,764 work-RAM writes over the driver's channel records
and track pointers, and 954,241 PSG, timer and interrupt-acknowledge
bus writes, each carrying its own master-cycle stamp. All three are
byte-identical, so the epilogue's stack, mapping and timing match
the machine write for write and cycle for cycle.

## The music sequencer

Bank `45` is the other half of the audio path. The timer driver streams
samples; the sequencer decides what they are, walking its voices once per
frame from `45:4015`, programming the PSG register file and maintaining
the per-voice records in `$2E00`-`$2FFF`.

Unlike the driver it is not flat: the tick calls sixteen subroutines, so
the port was incremental rather than one transcript. The subroutines went
first — the sample/envelope group the voice walk calls once per voice per
frame, the register programmer that pushes the result out, and the two
routines that decide what the notes are in the first place — and the tick
itself followed once it became clear it could be chunked:

| Entry | Admitted | What it does |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| `45:4015` | 1,933 of 3,599 | **The per-frame tick**, the bank's top level |
| `45:4044` | 1,208 of 7,724 | The head of its voice loop, and the chunk head |
| `45:409C` | 1,509 of 4,892 | Where the tick resumes after `4936` |
| `45:413B` | 3,599 of 3,599 | Where the tick resumes after `4142` |
| `45:48B4` | 72 of 72 | Sequence-data fetch, the shared command decoder |
| `45:4883` | 4,321 of 4,321 | Pitch/period update (inlines `48B4`) |
| `45:48F6` | 3,398 of 3,398 | Sweep accumulator update (inlines `48B4`) |
| `45:49A2` | 3,463 of 3,463 | Vibrato accumulator update |
| `45:49B6` | 1,671 of 1,671 | Voice-latch clear |
| `45:49F9` | 3,476 of 3,482 | Voice commit, which drives `$0800`/`$0804` |
| `45:4211` | 1,783 of 1,783 | The two pending-event gates |
| `45:4228` | 1,065 of 1,065 | Portamento stepper |
| `45:457A` | 2,219 of 2,299 | Command interpreter |
| `45:4142` | 3,216 of 3,604 | PSG register programmer, six voices plus the LFO |
| `45:4143` | 3,788 of 6,386 | The head of its channel loop, and the chunk head |

One walk serves all fifteen. Each stops in front of its own `RTS` and
hands the return back to the core, and any call the walk makes on the way
is inlined into the same transcript — which is why `48B4` is admitted only
72 times on its own: the other 25,896 calls to it are made from inside a
`4883` or `48F6` transcript and never reach the hook. Adding the tick did
the same thing one level up, which is why every subroutine count above
fell: 83,417 calls that used to arrive at a hook are now inlined into a
tick transcript. `4883` is 4,321 rather than 22,664 for exactly the
reason `48B4` was always 72. `457A` inlines four
callees the same way: the `$42BA` dispatch trampoline at `4569`, the
balance ramp `4522`, the note-to-period conversion `46D7`, and the forty
command handlers themselves.

The partial rows have three different reasons.

`$F5`, `$F7` and `$F9` are the timer driver's per-channel bank bytes, and
they are the only bytes in this bank's working set that another thread of
control writes: the driver sets one when it maps a channel's data bank and
clears it when the channel falls idle. `4142` reads all three, and the
amplitude commit at `45:49D2` reaches them through `$20F5,Y` — which both
`49B6` and `49F9` run, so every voice iteration of the tick touches it.
A transcript reads them from a snapshot
taken before it runs. The walk therefore declines unless the interrupt mask
is already set at entry — `4142` brackets each voice in PHP/SEI/PLP, so a
masked entry stays masked for the whole body — or the body finishes before
the next serviceable timer interrupt. That is a correctness boundary, not a
horizon heuristic: without it the transcript would act on a value the driver
had already changed.

`4142` sat at 1,792 of 3,604 under that guard for four slices, on the
arithmetic that a whole call averages 2,055 master cycles against a
5,068-cycle period. That is the same argument the tick was rejected on,
made one level down, and it fails the same way: `4142` is a loop too.
`4143` is its head, `41F7` closes it with a `JMP $4143`, and it now
chunks exactly as the tick does. The boundary is one ROM already
exposes rather than one the port invents — ROM's own `PLP` at `41F1`
has restored the caller's mask before control reaches `41F7`, so the top
of an iteration is somewhere the hardware may take an interrupt anyway.
15,972 of the route's 19,062 channel iterations now run inside a
transcript, worth 2,271,558 master cycles net.

The chunk margin is a bound taken from the instruction sequence rather
than from this route. Iterations come in two sizes: 390 master cycles
plain, and 2,190 for one that uploads a 32-byte waveform through `$0806`
at `41A3`-`41E1`, which only 186 of the 19,062 take. A margin of 500 was
measured as well, and it is better by 166,605 cycles — 0.013% — because
chunks run longer; it was not taken, because when a cheap-looking
iteration turns out to want a waveform the guard refuses the whole chunk
rather than shortening it, and the worst case of that constant is a tune
with heavy waveform switching that stops being admitted at all.

`457A` declines for a different reason: 45 of its calls hit the `$82`/`$92`
command, whose handler pops four bytes off the stack and jumps to `$40EE`,
abandoning the routine's own frame. A transcript that has to stop in front
of an `RTS` cannot express that. The other 35 are command handlers the
census route never reaches, left untranscribed rather than written blind.
Both would go away at a level where the escape is ordinary internal
control flow. The tick is now that level, and they have not gone away,
because a `457A` call reached from inside a tick transcript is inlined
rather than hooked: the 80 that still decline are the ones that arrive at
the hook on their own. Composing a caller removes a boundary; it does not
remove a path.

`4015`, `4044` and `409C` are partial for the third reason, and it is the
interesting one. See the section below.

Verification goes past the state census here, and it earned its keep twice.
The census is the direct oracle for an audio port, because it hashes the
audio, and it is unchanged. On top of that the regression compares three
artifacts against a pure-assembly run of the same route: 5,458,568 work-RAM
writes covering every voice record, the zero-page command pointer, the
timer driver's per-channel state and the stack, 699,251 PSG and timer
writes, and the census itself. All three are byte-identical, so the writes
match in value, address, order and master-cycle offset, not merely in end
state.

The stack range is the load-bearing part, and it has now caught two faults
the census could not. `JSR` pushes the return address *before* `ADDCYC(6)`,
while every other store in the walk lands on its instruction's final cycle,
so the first slice credited two stack writes 21 cycles late; widening the
work-RAM range to cover `$21E0`-`$21FF` found it on frame 148 of 3,600.
The third slice's `PLP` at `46AE` then published the caller's flags at its
checkpoint, so an interrupt landing on that exact instruction pushed the
pre-pop status — two differing bytes in 5.5 million, both on the stack,
with the census matching either way because the handler restores `P` on the
way out.

The bank is now 43,256,343 of 46,078,773 cycles native, 94%, up from
24,827,955. What is left is `45:4936`, the track loader `45:4BCC`, and
the chunk boundaries the tick and `4142` hand back.

### Chunking the per-frame tick

This section used to say that `45:4015` was not merely unwritten but
forbidden: its body is 12,667 master cycles, the timer period on this
route is 5,068, it reads driver state, and so a transcript builder for it
would decline 3,599 calls out of 3,599. Every number in that sentence is
correct and the conclusion was wrong, for the same reason the identical
claim about `00:EAA7` was wrong one slice earlier: **the body that has to
fit is a transcript's, and a transcript is a free choice wherever the
body is a loop.**

The tick's body is a loop. `45:4044` is its head, `45:40FF` closes it
with a `JMP $4044`, and the profile gives the shape exactly: 32,391
iterations across 3,599 calls, 28,792 continuing and 3,599 exiting, one
clean exit per call. One iteration composed is 1,137 master cycles.

That is the whole difference, and it is worth being precise about what
kind of limit was lifted. The driver-state rule is **a deadline, not a
cap**. A cap on transcript length is met by writing less; a deadline is
met by doing less *before it*. So the walk stops at the loop's closing
jump once one more iteration would not fit inside the horizon it was
handed, the core takes the jump and the interrupt, and the next chunk
starts against a fresh horizon. 24,667 of the route's 32,391 voice
iterations now run inside a transcript.

The recorded rejection also named the wrong read, and that is the part
worth carrying to the next screen. It said the voice loop reaches
`45:4148`, `LDA $F5,X`, three times per call. It does not reach it at
all: the bank's only `JSR $4142` is at `45:4138`, *past* the loop's
closing jump, and `4148`'s 10,812 executions are three per call of
`4142` rather than three per iteration of the tick. Every branch in
`4044`-`4101` targets an address inside the loop or the exit at `4102`.
The tick does read driver state per iteration, but through `49D2`, which
nobody had looked at. A correct conclusion resting on a reason that would
not survive being checked is still worth re-checking when the method
changes.

`4015` and `4044` are admitted on roughly half their arrivals because a
chunk boundary is, by construction, the moment the horizon is nearly
spent: the entry immediately after one often cannot fit an iteration
either and hands that iteration to assembly. That is the cost of the
technique and it is already priced into the figures above.

The slice moved 14,664,984 cycles into bank 45 and cost 1,647,495
elsewhere, a bill of 11.2%, for a net `+13,017,489` and 41.61% to
**42.62%**. The bill was measured with a `--registry-exclude` run rather
than estimated, and it is worth reporting how clean it was: the whole of
it is `00:FC00`, the sound driver, whose transcripts are the thing a
longer tick transcript displaces. **No other routine on the route moved
by a single cycle.**

## The palette shadow buffer

Gameplay code never writes the VCE. It keeps thirty-two 32-byte blocks of
packed nine-bit GRB in a shadow buffer at `$27B9`-`$2BB8`, marks the blocks
it changed in `$2CCC`-`$2CEB`, and `00:EB30` pushes the dirty ones out to
the VCE ports once a frame. Five routines are now native, reached through
seven registered addresses, and they are the port's first move into the
video paths. They are also where transcripts stopped handing their `RTS`
back to the core: **every walk here ends past its own return**, which is
worth 14,369 returns and 301,749 master cycles on this route and is
described under *Absorbing the return* below.

| Address | What it is | Native calls | Native master cycles |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: |
| `00:EB30` + `00:EB4D` | The upload: dirty blocks out to the VCE, and the whole-palette arm either side of its `TIA` | 3,599 and 29, both of both | 8,010,945 |
| `00:E9B6` | Block interpolator: one crossfade step over sixteen colours | 672 | 6,563,058 |
| `00:EBA5` | Block stager: a 32-byte copy plus the dirty flags | 1,121 | 2,653,407 |
| `00:E93C` | The sixteen-bit compare the fade drivers branch on | 8,969 | 946,719 |
| `00:EAA7` | Fade-to-black stepper: one step over all thirty-two blocks | 8, in 101 chunks | 1,652,688 |

The last column is the deployed registry's own measured credit
(`--native-cycle-census`), not the routines' cost in an assembly-only run —
`ROUTINE_MAP.md` and `EMULATOR_ELIMINATION.md` carry that inventory, and
the two are close but no longer identical, because each transcript now
carries its own return. Earlier revisions of this table quoted the
inventory figures here and one derived `00:EB30` + `00:EB4D` total;
all five rows are measured.

The bottom four are admitted on every call, which is unusual enough to
explain. They are leaves — no `JSR`, no device port, and nothing another
thread of control writes — so there is no horizon to miss and no snapshot
to go stale. An interrupt landing inside one is handled the way it always
is, by a checkpoint.

The interpolator is the interesting one. Each colour's three channels are
summed and compared against the step counter at `$2CEC`; colours whose
total falls below it are skipped, which is what staggers the fade, and the
rest have exactly one channel nudged a step toward the target — whichever
of the three differences is largest. The differences are computed with
`SBC` and no clamp, so a channel the shadow has overshot wraps to a large
unsigned value and is treated as furthest away, which is what drives it
back.

`00:EB30` is the first routine in the project to drive a device other
than the VDC and the PSG. It walks the thirty-two dirty flags, and for
each block it points the colour table at `$0402`/`$0403` and pushes
sixteen colours through `$0404`/`$0405`. Twenty-nine of its 3,599 calls
take a second arm instead, where a single `TIA $27B9, $0404, $0400`
moves all 1,024 bytes at once — 1,024 writes against a channel that
carries 512, and a block transfer has no interior boundary a chunk could
stop at.

For a long time that arm was recorded as declined, and it was the routine
that was written down rather than the instruction. The `TIA` is one
instruction of eleven. Above it are four flag tests and two stores that
zero the colour-table address; below it are two stores that clear the
upload requests and an `RTS`. So the arm splits the way the interrupt
dispatcher did: `00:EB30` walks to the transfer and stops, the core
executes it with all 1,024 of its port writes at its own cycle, and
`00:EB4D` — a seventh registered address that nothing in the game jumps
to — finishes the arm. `00:EB30` is now admitted on every call.

**What that buys is admission, not cycles, and the difference from the
dispatcher is worth stating.** The `TIA` is 625,095 of the arm's 627,618
master cycles; the six instructions the split moved into transcripts are
2,523, and the whole change is `+3,915` on the deployed registry. At
`00:E058` the unportable instruction was 17% of its block and splitting
moved 640,374. Reading a wall as an instruction is always the right
reading, but it only pays when the instruction is not itself the cost.

### Absorbing the return

Every transcript in the project used to stop in front of its `RTS` and let
the core execute it. That cost 21 master cycles a call at each end, it was
written down as an overhead of the hybrid that no amount of porting could
remove, and it was the single largest category of remaining emulated
cycles outside the frame-sync wait — 13.3M on this route. It was not
structural. Three things had to hold, and all three did:

- **The core does not know what routine a transcript was entered at.**
  Nothing relates `resume_logical_pc` to the entry address, at the install
  site or at a checkpoint; the core assigns it to `PC` and restarts its
  loop. The host's one related check is a consistency check — the reported
  bank must be what `output_registers.mpr[resume >> 13]` maps that segment
  to — so a walk resolves the bank through its own MPRs rather than
  assuming its own module's.
- **The return address is data the entry already carries.** The stack is
  ordinary work RAM at `$2100`-`$21FF`. `RTS` pops the low byte from
  `$2100 + S + 1` and the high from `$2100 + S + 2` and lands on that word
  plus one, `JSR` having pushed the address of its own last byte. Read it
  out of the staged work RAM, put `S + 2` in `output_registers`.
- **Interrupt delivery does not move.** The final checkpoint samples at its
  declared offset, advances to its end, assigns the caller's address and
  pops the frame — all before the loop reaches `DcSelectInterrupt`. So the
  boundary after the absorbed `RTS` is a real boundary with the caller's
  address in `PC` and `S` already popped, which is what the machine does.

`RTS` is 6 cycles plus the one `LastCycle` charges after it samples: 21
master with the interrupt sampled 3 back, needing no special `sample_back`.
And it **emits no write**, which is what makes this checkable instead of
merely arguable — absorbing one is not allowed to move anything in any
trace. Nothing moved. The census, the work-RAM trace and the VCE bus trace
are byte-identical to the stop-in-front version, in the family regression
and in the whole-registry census, and an independent Debug run of the same
route reproduced all three.

On this family that is 14,369 returns and **+301,749 native master cycles**
— 671,640,741 → 671,942,490, which `14,369 × 21` closes exactly. The seven
return addresses fell from 302,628 emulated cycles to **63**: three
executions reached by interpretation rather than through a transcript.

"Not allowed to move anything in any trace" held for every trace the family
regression takes and failed for one it does not.
`--routine-call-trace` completes an observed call on an emulated `RTS`
boundary, and absorbing the return deletes the boundary it waits for — so
the call stayed open until an unrelated ancestor returned at the same stack
pointer. `00:E93C` traced afterwards reported 8,966 calls rather than
8,969, the first returning to `01:CAB6` after 53,486,487 cycles rather than
to `00:F0FE` after 78, with 38,057,951 writes against a routine that makes
12,905. A transcript now declares the return it absorbs so the host can
close the call where the assembly closed it; the three artifacts are again
byte-identical whether the routine is emulated, replaced, or replaced and
absorbing, which is what `dc_rom_absorbed_return_trace` requires on every
run. See *An absorbed return has to be declared* in
`NATIVE_PORT_WORKFLOW.md`.

One measured side effect, benign and to be expected wherever this is done
next. Nested installs rose 295,164 → 295,258. Extending a transcript by one
checkpoint means the instruction before the `RTS` no longer ends it, so
there is one more boundary at which an interrupt can arrive while the
transcript is still active, and its handler nests. Delivery lands on the
same boundary at the same cycle either way, which is why nothing moved.
Depth stayed at 3 of a permitted 4 and no replacement was refused.

**Rolled out across bank `03`**, which was the largest remaining pool of
emulated returns. Seven more families absorb theirs — `03:7F57`, `03:7378`,
`03:6054`, `03:65A4`, `03:78C7`, `03:7CAF`, `03:60A6` and `03:7B7A` — taking
the bank from 2,552,511 emulated return cycles to 1,498,290 and the route
from 8,957,280 to 7,599,885. The registry goes 671,942,490 → 673,091,757.
The mechanism is shared now (`detail::absorb_return`), so a family costs one
call per arm that reaches a return, and nested installs rose 295,258 →
295,740 with nothing refused.

What is *not* a target is the more useful half of that. An emulated `RTS` can
only be absorbed by the walk that owns it, so a bank's return ranking is not a
work queue: `03:603E` is the second-largest row left in the bank at 137,607
cycles and belongs to an unported parent at `03:6005`. Rank by ported owner.

**Bank `08` then cost two edits and paid more than all eight of those.** Its
1,726,353 cycles of emulated returns are 95% two addresses, and both belong to
walks that were already native: `08:41AB` ends the sprite emitter `08:40DF` on
every one of its 73,818 calls, and `08:40DE` ends the SAT tail clear `08:40C3`
on the 3,492 of its 4,334 arrivals that run Y down rather than spend the write
budget. The bank falls to 92,742 and the route to 5,966,274; the registry goes
673,091,757 → 674,715,267, 52.21% of the route and 94.97% outside the
frame-sync wait. The same "rank by ported owner" test explains both halves of
the bank: everything left in it — `08:5E4C`, `08:5976`, `08:5A69` — returns
from a routine that has no transcript.

**Bank `02` was both shapes at once and took twenty-one edits.** Its 1,636,158
cycles were the largest pool left, and the head of it is one family: the
Sorcerer tick stops on three of its own returns, and 02:5903 alone was 384,024
master cycles — the single largest emulated instruction anywhere on the route.
One edit took all three. The other eighteen families behind it are bank `03`'s
shape, a return apiece. The bank falls to 321,630 and the route to 4,651,746;
the registry goes 674,715,267 → 676,133,460, **52.32%** of the route and
**95.17%** outside the frame-sync wait.

Three of those families are *atomic* — no checkpoints, nothing inside them at
which an interrupt could be taken — and for them absorbing is not free. The
horizon valve declines an atomic body that would reach a user event or a
serviceable timer interrupt, so twenty-one more cycles cost 311 calls their
whole body before the guard was written. `absorbed_return_fits_atomically` asks
first and hands the return back when the room is not there; coverage returned
to 929/1,055, 926/1,085, 873/1,260 and 1,015/1,288, and the route still has
none declined at a horizon. An interruptible transcript has no such limit,
which is why eighteen of the twenty-one families never had to ask.

**Bank `03`'s remainder took twenty-two edits and needed a new distinction to
take the first two.** `03:6966`, the collision-table interpreter, is the
busiest routine on the route and its two returns were 442,578 cycles; `03:7FB1`,
the collision response, was another 110,856. Neither could absorb inside itself.
Both are shared: the same function serves the registered entry *and* the sites
that run it in line — the Sorcerer tick's two calls, `03:7F57`, `03:73AC` — and
every one of those reads `return_instruction` to learn which `RTS` the child
stopped on so that it can transcribe the return itself. So the absorption
belongs to the *entry* rather than to the routine.
`replace_collision_table_entry` and `replace_collision_response_entry` are what
the registry dispatches; the composition sites go on calling the routines
directly and see what they always saw. Twenty more families behind them are the
ordinary shape, a return apiece, and eleven of those rows go to zero. The bank
falls 1,498,290 → 624,837 and the route 4,651,746 → 3,778,293; the registry goes
676,133,460 → 677,038,098, **52.39%** of the route and **95.29%** outside the
frame-sync wait.

**This is also where the technique's ceiling became visible.** Absorbing a
return removes it only from calls whose transcript reaches its own end, and a
transcript that an interrupt cuts short finishes under the core — including its
`RTS`. Every one of the interpreter's 14,307 registry entries absorbs (native
cycles rise by exactly 14,307 × 21), and `03:6A37` and `03:6988` still execute
8,381 times, because 12% of those entries and every arrival through a cut
composition finish emulated. The rows that go to zero are the short families;
the ones that leave a residue are the long ones. Nothing about the ranking says
which is which, so the residue is worth reading after the edit rather than
predicted before it.

**Bank `01` was the largest pool and the cheapest of the four, because two
edits took a third of it.** `replace_collision_tail` and
`replace_actor_geometry_leaf` are walking interpreters: each is dispatched for
many registered entries and each ends by stopping in front of whichever address
in a fixed list it walked into. Absorbing at that one place covers thirteen
returns — 213,939 master cycles for the first and 158,472 for the second — and
neither routine is composed by anything, so the return belongs inside the
routine and needs no wrapper. What the list needed was reading rather than
trusting: eleven of the tail's twenty-five stops are `RTS` and only ten of
those are returns. `01:CBF9` is the tail dispatch at the end of the confirmed-contact
dispatcher, where `01:CBF4` and `01:CBF8` push a vector out of a table and the
`RTS` jumps to it. The word it pops is one the routine wrote two instructions
earlier, so no call completes there and there is nothing to absorb.

Three of bank `01`'s routines needed the entry wrapper, and the coroutine
scheduler at `01:C5C9` is the reason the technique now has a fifth regression.
Twenty-seven walks in `native_replacements_rules.cpp` run it in line and adopt
its `return_instruction` and `body_master_cycles` as their own, several of them
testing that the body is exactly 159 cycles — so a scheduler that ran past its
own return would have made every one of those compositions fail its guard and
fall back silently. It is also the first absorption whose frame is not where the
entry's stack pointer says: its first two instructions are the `PLA`s that take
its own return address off the stack to record where it was called from, so the
`RTS` reads its caller's caller's frame, at $2100+S+3 and +4 rather than the
+1 and +2 every other absorption reads. Reading the wrong pair there would not
look wrong — it would return to the address the routine was called from, which
is a real address the machine would go on executing. That is why `dc_rom_absorbed_return_trace` now
runs over five routines.

The bank falls 1,060,104 → 187,590 and the route 3,778,293 → 2,905,779; the
registry goes 677,038,098 → 677,903,067, **52.45%** of the route and **95.41%**
outside the frame-sync wait. Twenty-three rows go to zero.

**And bank `01` is where the technique's second limit showed up**, in the one
family held back rather than shipped. The actor scan loop at `01:C510` is both
long enough to be cut short often and re-entered at the same stack depth, and
the two together defeat the observer's bookkeeping. A transaction stages the
return it promises to absorb when it is installed, and the promise is redeemed
at the boundary the machine lands on afterwards; nothing withdraws it when the
transcript never gets that far. A later arrival at the same address and depth
then redeems the stale promise and closes a call with an abandoned transcript's
registers — 82,201 of 176,861 calls reporting the wrong `Y`, and 42,753 the
wrong `MPR4`/`MPR5`, with every cycle column still correct. Withdrawing the
promise when the core executes the `RTS` itself fixes three calls in eight and
no more, because a cut-short scan usually leaves through the dispatch at
`01:C52C` and never reaches `01:C52B` at all. Doing it properly means the core
telling the runtime when it abandons a transcript, which is a fork patch. Until
then `01:C52B` keeps its 73,536 master cycles, and it is the largest single
thing left in the bank.

The `JSR` half is the same argument from the other side and is not new: a
caller's walk that steps into the callee is composition, which `01:CB48`
has been doing since it was written — push the frame, inline the callee's
transcript, add two back to `S`. What that needs is a *ported caller*, so
it is a per-family question rather than a sweep.

Admitting it took a two-line widening on each side of the boundary: the
VCE register file was added to the replacement write channel's
allowlist, and to the core's wait-state compensation. The second is
what makes the timing come out. `IOWrite` steals a CPU cycle for both
the VDC ports and the VCE, and the interruptible path already advanced
to the cycle *before* a VDC write so that steal landed inside the
declared offset; the VCE was outside that test, so the first run of the
transcript ran three master cycles long per colour — 102 per block,
accumulating all frame. The write values and their order were already
right at that point. Only the clock was wrong, which is exactly the
class of fault a value-only comparison would have passed.

`00:EAA7` is the one that had to change shape to fit. It is 205,012
master cycles a call — 512 colours, 5,300 work-RAM writes against a
channel that carries 512 — and was set aside twice for being past the
cap however the transcript was arranged. That was true of a transcript
per call and false of the routine. It **chunks**, the technique the VRAM
decoder introduced: `00:EAB0`, the head of its colour loop, is a second
registered address, and a walk stops in front of the `JMP $EAB0` that
closes the loop once a channel is nearly full. The core takes the jump,
lands on `00:EAB0` and calls back. Eight calls become 101 transcripts,
and each boundary costs the twelve master cycles of the jump.

Where the decoder needed a lookahead — its stream records vary in
length, so it reads the next record's command byte before committing to
it — a colour is a fixed seventeen writes, so the stepper decides on the
counters alone. Chunking is cheaper the more uniform the loop body is.

Two neighbours stay emulated, and the reason given here was wrong.
`00:E94D` and `00:EA77`, the two fade drivers, both call the frame-sync
spin at `00:E321`, and this paragraph used to say that made a transcript
span the twenty-one frames they wait through. It does not — no more than
the `TIA` above makes one span 1,024 writes. A walk stops in front of the
`JSR`, the core takes the spin, and the return address is the next entry
point; the crossfade driver would chunk on its passes exactly as
`00:EAA7` chunks on its colours. It has a second such instruction of its
own, the `TII $27B9, $27BA, $03FF` at `00:E950`, which is 1,023 work-RAM
writes and 18,465 master cycles, and it splits the same way.

They are left alone for a different reason, and it is a reason about
evidence rather than capability: this route enters `00:E94D` **once** and
`00:EA77` eight times, for 138,582 and 1,455 master cycles. Every entry
point in the table above is admitted on hundreds or thousands of calls,
which is what lets the regression claim a transcript is state-generic
rather than a transcription of one observed path. One sample cannot make
that claim, and 0.011% of the route is not worth weakening the bar for.

Verification is the two-run diff, now over three artifacts: 7,369,685
work-RAM writes covering the shadow buffer, the dirty flags, the
zero-page scratch and the stack; 115,688 VCE register writes; and the
state census. All three are byte-identical to a pure-assembly run of the
same route. Nothing in this slice pushes, so every stack write in that
trace belongs to surrounding code — which is exactly what makes it a
good check on the cycle counts.

## The bank-switching call wrappers

Every cross-bank call in the game goes through one of five small wrappers.
The caller leaves a physical bank in `$30` and an entry offset in `$2F`; a
wrapper maps the bank into an MPR slot, jumps through the callee's `$40xx`
table, and unmaps it on the way out. `$1E`, `$1F`, `$20` and `$21` are the
zero-page shadows of MPR2 through MPR5, and keeping the shadow and the
register in step is what makes the idiom re-entrant: a wrapper that
preempts another restores both from its own stack frame.

Nine entry points are native, admitted on every one of 52,911 calls:

| --- | --- | ---: | ---: |
| `00:E241` | Banked call, prologue: save MPR2/MPR3 and map `$30` | 11,599 | 1,322,286 |
| `00:E255` | Banked call, epilogue: take the result, restore MPR2/MPR3 | 11,599 | 1,043,910 |
| `00:E262` | The trampoline: build `$40xx` in `$2CBE`/`$2CBF` | 11,671 | 700,260 |
| `00:ECC6` | The VBlank handler's music call: map banks `$45`-`$47` | 3,503 | 567,486 |
| `00:E219` | Map the fixed bank pair for the mode at `$55` | 3,523 | 422,760 |
| `00:ECE2` | Restore MPR2-MPR4 after it | 3,503 | 409,851 |
| `00:E20D` | Map `$30` into MPR4/MPR5 | 1,861 | 133,992 |
| `00:ECF2` | Point `$47`/`$48` at `$4003` and enter the sequencer | 3,503 | 126,108 |
| `00:E200` | The asset resolver's tail | 1,861 | 122,826 |

Four of those are heads and five are the addresses a wrapper's call comes
back to, because a wrapper exists to call something and the transcript has
to stop in front of that call. Two of the stops are indirect jumps,
`00:E26E` and `00:ECFA`, and the walk stops in front of both rather than
resolving them: each reads a vector the same transcript just wrote, so
handing the jump back to the core costs six instructions of coverage and
removes the only place this module would have to compute a resume bank.

This is the first slice whose transcripts change the bank mapping. `TAM`
is expressible because `CpuRegisters` carries all eight `MPR` slots and a
checkpoint applies them, so the only real question was whether anything
here reads state an interrupt handler leaves changed. Nothing does. The
VBlank handler's own wrapper at `00:ECC6` is the sole interrupt-context
writer of `$1E`-`$21` in the whole game, and it restores all three from
the stack before it returns; the timer driver never touches them. That was
settled from a work-RAM write trace of the four addresses before any code
was written — the same two-query screen that ruled out `45:4015`.

Verification is the two-run diff over 6,389,776 work-RAM writes and the
state census, both byte-identical to a pure-assembly run of the same route.
The whole stack page is in scope rather than a window of it: this family is
nothing but stack-balanced save and restore, and its pushes land wherever
the caller left `S`, spanning `$21E2` through `$21FD` on this route.

`00:ED5E` is the fifth wrapper and is deliberately absent. It is entered
with two of its four stack slots already pushed by its caller, so its entry
contract belongs to the caller rather than the routine, and it is 23,000
master cycles across 72 calls — 0.4% of this family.

## The VDC interrupt handler

`00:E058` is the display interrupt. It reads the VDC status register at
`$0000` into `$01` and dispatches on it: the VBlank bit sends it to
`00:E0EB`, everything else to `00:E136`. For four slices the dispatcher
was recorded as blocked in its entirety, on the grounds that its whole
decision is a byte from a device register. That was the wrong unit. One
*instruction* is unportable — see **The one instruction that is not**
below — and both sides of it are now native.

Nine entry points are native, and every one of them is now admitted on
every arrival under the deployed registry. Until the core could nest a
replacement, an interrupt that arrived while a main-thread transcript
was suspended ran the handler emulated: `00:E136` reached 1,180 of
3,599 frames and `00:E195` 3,159. The chain gained 2,864,880 cycles
when that rule went, without a line of it changing:

| Address | What it is | Native calls | Master cycles |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: |
| `00:E195` | Pad reader: poll five ports, publish held state and edges | 3,599 of 3,599 | 6,854,091 |
| `00:E136` | Non-VBlank arm: reprogram the split, tick `$222A` | 3,599 of 3,599 | 1,160,913 |
| `00:E0EB` | VBlank prologue: program VDC `$08`/`$07`/`$05` from the RAM shadows | 2,748 of 2,748 | 544,104 |
| `00:E05F` | Dispatcher tail: stash the status byte, pick the arm from `$54` | 7,198 of 7,198 | 381,246 |
| `00:E123` | Epilogue after the music call: restore MPR6 and unwind | 3,212 of 3,212 | 337,260 |
| `00:E058` | Dispatcher head: push `P`, `A`, `X`, `Y` and stop at the read | 7,198 of 7,198 | 259,128 |

`00:E195` is worth more than the other eight together, and it is the first
routine here that *reads* a device. It was recorded as out of reach for
four slices on the rule that a replacement callback cannot see a device
register — the same rule that was thought to block `00:E058`.

The rule is about the callback, not about the value. The byte the joypad
answers with is a function of two things a walk can know: the pad state
for the frame, and the `SEL` and `CLR` latch lines, which this routine
drives itself through `$1000`. The latch is pure bookkeeping — the store
at `E198` clears `CLR` whatever it held, so the store at `E19D` always
resets the multitap cursor and the loop steps it once per pass, which
makes the cursor the loop counter and needs nothing assumed about the
state the port was left in. The pad state is the one genuinely new
input, and `RoutineEntry` now carries it, taken from the same field the
core answers `$1000` reads from, so the value the transcript predicts and
the value assembly would have read are one object rather than two that
have to be kept in step.

`$1000` joined the write channel for the four latch stores, so the port
is left where assembly would have left it; scanning the ROM for absolute
stores to it finds eight, four of them this routine's and the other four
never executed. Verification is the pad bytes at `$200E`-`$201C` and the
`$1000` writes themselves, both byte-identical to assembly in value,
address, order and master-cycle offset across all 3,599 calls.

**A device read is not automatically a wall.** It is not a wall when the
value depends only on the outside world and on lines the routine itself
set, which is this case. It is not a wall merely because the value is
device state either, once that device has a native model with a
non-disturbing read. What makes one a wall is below.

### The one instruction that is not

`00:E05C` is `LDA $0000`, and it is the only instruction in this handler
the port cannot take. The reason recorded for four slices was the one
above — the VDC status register reports what the display is doing, and a
callback cannot see it — and that reason turned out not to be the binding
one. It stopped being true of the *value* the day the VDC became a native
model: `PceVdc::read` already takes a `peek` flag that answers without
disturbing anything, so the entry could have carried the byte the same
way it carries the frame's pad state.

What does not lift is that here the read *is* the acknowledge. Reading
`$0000` clears every latched status source at once and is the only way
the VDC's interrupt line falls (`src/video/pce_vdc.cpp`). The replacement
contract is a **write** channel, and a write to `$0000` selects a
register rather than acknowledging one, so there is no way for a
transcript to perform that side effect at the cycle the instruction
performs it. A transcript that predicted the byte and skipped the read
would return to a core whose interrupt had never been taken, and the
handler would be re-entered immediately.

So the rule to carry forward is narrower than the one it replaces, and
it is about the instruction rather than the routine:

> A device read can be transcribed when the walk can compute the value.
> It cannot be transcribed when the read *changes* the device, because
> the transcript has no channel for the change.

Splitting there costs one instruction and recovers the rest. The four
register saves above it touch only the stack page; below it the byte the
dispatcher branches on is already in the accumulator, so the device is
never wanted again. Of the dispatcher's 769,938 master cycles on the
census route, 640,374 are now native and 129,564 are the `LDA` — the
core still executes it, at its own cycle, with its acknowledge intact.

Every walk that reaches an `RTI` stops one instruction short of it, in
front of the `PLP` that restores the interrupted status byte, and that is
the whole lesson of the slice that ported the bodies. The core samples the IRQ at `LastCycle`, which for `PLP` runs
*before* the pop; a checkpoint carries one register set and samples with
it, so a transcript covering `PLP` samples with the restored mask instead
of the one the instruction ran under. Assembly services a timer interrupt
between that `PLP` and the `RTI`; the first draft of this slice swallowed
it, and the state census caught it. This is the same fault the sound
driver's `CLI` produced from the other direction, and the general rule is
now stated once: an instruction that changes `I` cannot be summarised by a
checkpoint that publishes its result.

That mattered because the handler does not run masked throughout, which
is not obvious: an interrupt sequence sets `I`, and nothing in the handler
clears it. Bank `45` does. Three of its critical sections end with an
unconditional `CLI` rather than restoring the caller's mask — `45:4872`,
`45:49F2` and `45:47EE`, 376 executions between them — so the music tick
below `00:ECC6` hands the handler back with interrupts enabled. Counting
interrupt entries in an assembly write trace of the stack page puts 78 of
them inside these six walks, every one at or after `00:E123` and none
before a `JSR $ECC6`.

The shadowing bill is small in spite of that: 64 driver entries and
36,330 cycles, 2.7% of the slice's own 1,350,720, because the unmasked
stretch is the short one.

Verification is the two-run diff against pure assembly over three
artifacts: 4,989,037 work-RAM writes including the whole stack page,
2,036,234 writes to the VDC's three base ports censused directly with
`--bus-write-range`, and the state census. The stack page is what makes
the interrupt fault visible at all, because a swallowed interrupt moves
where its handler pushes.

`00:E3C0`–`E47C` is one idiom three times over: point a VDC or VCE write

The decoder is the whole prize, and it is the first thing in the port that
does not fit in a transcript. One call is 167,000 master cycles, 3,400 port
writes and 12,400 instructions, against a channel of 512 writes and 2,048
checkpoints. So it chunks. Each routine has two entry points — its head, and
the head of the loop — and a transcript ends in front of the backward branch
that closes the loop. The core executes that branch, lands on the loop head,
and calls the replacement again for the next chunk. Nothing in the core
changed to allow this: the hook already fires on any fetch at a registered
address, and the replacement contract already ends by handing one instruction
back. The 41 decoder calls become 41 head transcripts and 326 continuations,
and the leak is the branch itself — eighteen master cycles a chunk, 5,868 on
the route.

Where to chunk is a budget decision, not a fixed count. `00:E479` reads the
next stream record's command byte before committing to it and stops if that
record would not fit in what is left of either channel, so a transcript never
abandons work it has already transcribed. A record is at most 127 words on
this route — 258 writes — so the first record of a chunk always fits and the
walk always makes progress. That is the property that matters: a chunk that
made no progress would loop for ever.

The other precondition was shared state. `$2F`/`$30` is the game's
general-purpose source pointer, written from some 340 sites across eight
banks, and a transcript that reads it is only sound if nothing writes it
underneath. That was measured rather than argued: bracketing each of the 41
calls between its header's `$06` store at `00:E422` and its last data-port
write, all 23,300 writes to `$2D`-`$30`, `$37` and `$38` inside those windows
come from the decoder itself, and none from interrupt context.

The shadowing bill runs the other way here. The VDC handler's transcripts
shadowed the sound driver from inside interrupt context; these are main-thread
transcripts averaging 18,000 cycles, more than three timer periods, so the
interrupts that land inside them cannot reach their own hooks. 6,908,844
native cycles cost 317,196 elsewhere across 956 entries — 130,122 in the sound
driver, 165,123 in the music sequencer, 21,348 in the VDC handler and its
bank wrapper, 603 in the palette upload. Net `+6,591,648`. At 4.6% of gross
that sits between the bank wrappers' 15.6% and the VDC handler's 2.7%, and
it is the cheapest bill any main-thread slice has paid.

One of the 367 transcripts is abandoned partway through a repeat record and
the assembly finishes it: 45 loop iterations and one record tail out of
6.9M cycles. That is the core's checkpoint guard doing its job — it resumes a
suspended transcript only when the program counter, stack pointer, registers
and all eight MPR slots match what the checkpoint published, and otherwise
drops it and lets the assembly run. The fallback is by design and the census
is unaffected.

Verification is the two-run diff against pure assembly over three artifacts:
6,036,242 work-RAM writes covering the decoder's shared zero page and the
whole stack page, 2,151,922 writes to the VDC and VCE register files censused
directly with `--bus-write-range`, and the state census.

## The tilemap upload, and an instruction a checkpoint cannot express

`00:F261`–`F4F1` is the other half of the graphics driver's port work and

the transcript.

## Composition, and what a longer transcript costs the driver

The sweep at `01:CB48` no longer stops in front of `JSR $CC1A`. It
transcribes the call, splices the scanner's own transcript in where the core
would have run it, transcribes the `RTS`, and hands control back at `01:CB61`
with the scan already answered. Nothing about the scanner changed: the same
`replace_collision_scan_vertical_reject` runs, on a staged work RAM that
carries the sweep's own writes, and its checkpoints and writes are shifted by
the cycle the call landed on. It is the pattern `01:CC1A` already used for
`01:CCD5`, applied one level up.

83,905 of the scanner's 85,715 calls compose. The 1,810 that do not are the
hit path, which leaves the scanner from the middle of itself at `CCD2` or
`CCD4`; there is no `RTS` to transcribe from there, so the walk rolls its
attempt back and ends in front of the call exactly as before. `01:CC1A` stays
installed for them.

The call boundary is gone. `01:CB5E` falls from 1,795,419 master cycles to
17,622 and `01:CC40` from 1,797,309 to 19,776; what is left is the hit path
and a few interrupt re-entries. `01:CB48` absorbs the scanner and becomes the
largest single native address on the route at 7.96% of all master cycles,
ahead of the sprite emitter.

The gross gain is exactly what the tax predicted — 83,905 × 42 = 3,524,010 —
and this is the one case where predicting it was safe, because a `JSR` and an
`RTS` cost 21 each whatever surrounds them. The bill was not safe to predict.
A composed walk is a longer transcript, and a longer transcript shadows the
timer driver for more of its length: `00:FC00` loses 1,184 admitted entries
and 683,271 native cycles, and sixteen other routines lose 11,790 between
them. 695,061 in total, 19.7% of gross, against the collision frame's 21.0%.
Net `+2,828,949`.

So composition pays for itself roughly four times over here, and it is worth
being clear about which half of that is transferable. The 42 cycles are
structural and will be there at every call boundary in the port. The
four-to-one ratio is not: it holds because this call sits in a loop whose
body is short, so the tax was a large share of the region. A call around a
long body is worth composing for other reasons, but not for this one.

## Three leaves in bank 01, and a rejection that did not survive the method

The three largest addresses left on the route with no native entry anywhere
inside them turned out to have nothing in common but a shape. They are not
one routine, they do not call each other, and two of them belong to different
subsystems. What they share is that each is straight-line integer arithmetic
over work RAM and zero page, each ends in an `RTS`, and none of them calls
anything, reads a device register or changes a bank. That is the whole
specification a transcript needs, so they went in as one walk.

| Entry | What it is | Calls | Native cycles |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: |
| `01:C8F3` | Path-length accumulator | 2,340 of 2,340 | 1,507,932 |
| `01:DAF8` | Direction-octant classifier | 2,139 of 2,139 | 1,450,425 |
| `01:C969` | Range-squared guard | 2,993 of 2,993 | 107,748 |

`01:C8F3` takes the absolute value of the two signed sixteen-bit velocity
components in `$3400,X`/`$3480,X` and `$3500,X`/`$3580,X`, adds them, shifts
the sum left four times and adds the result into a twenty-four-bit running
total whose top byte saturates at 4. `01:DAF8` saturates the signed
difference between two actors on each axis to a byte, records the two signs
and an ordering swap in three bits of `$45`, splits the octant three ways
against 3:2 and 12:2 ratios, and reads the answer out of a thirty-two-entry
table in the bank's own ROM page. `01:C969` never runs its body on this
route at all: the sixteen-bit counter it guards on is zero at all 2,993
entries, so every call is three instructions and an `RTS`, and the walk hands
the other arm back to the core rather than transcribing a path it has never
seen.

**`01:DAF8` had been screened and rejected twice**, both times on the grounds
that its cycle, write and result populations were "mixed rather than exposing
a stable state-derived return". That was the correct answer for the method in
use then, which had to find a return value a replacement could predict from
the entry state. An instruction-boundary transcript predicts nothing -- it
executes the branches the data selects -- so a mixed population is not an
obstacle to it, it is just two exits. Both are transcribed and the calls
divide 1,459 to 680 between them. Old rejections are worth re-reading when
the method changes; this one had been sitting for several slices.

The screen came back with no foreign writer at all. Of the 358 instructions
that write any address these three read, exactly four ever write inside a
call window, and all four are `01:C8F3`'s own stores. The Sorcerer tick
before it had one in-window writer and it was its own callee; these have
none, because they have no callee.

The bill was 626,298, 20.4% of 3,066,105 gross, and it is the cleanest
attribution any slice has produced: two routines account for all of it. The
timer driver lost 1,082 admitted entries and 617,673 native cycles, and the
VBlank body at `00:E136` lost 23 entries and 8,625.

That makes six measurements -- 15.6%, 18.1%, 21.0%, 19.7%, 17.8%, 20.4% --
and the per-event numbers under them still barely move. The driver costs 571
to 577 native cycles per displaced entry, and one entry is displaced for
every 2,775 to 3,300 master cycles a transcript holds the core, so the bill
is close to `gross / 3,000 × 575`, about 19%. Both of this slices figures
sit at the near end of their bands, which is the direction short transcripts
should push: three routines averaging 700 cycles a call displace the driver
slightly more often per cycle transcribed than one routine averaging 9,700.

Two caveats keep it an estimate. The six points span three percentage points
either side, which is 100,000 cycles on a slice this size. And the
displacement rate is 1.7 times the naive figure: the timer fires every 5,068
master cycles, so a transcript covering N cycles should overlap N/5,068 of
them, not N/3,000. Part of that is the shadow window being wider than the
transcript, which accounts for about a fifth of the excess; the rest is
measured, consistent across four slices, and not yet explained. Use the rule
to decide whether a region is worth starting and keep measuring the bill with
a `--registry-exclude` run before reporting what a slice delivered.

What is left of the three regions is 158,955 cycles, and 158,472 of it is the
four `RTS` instructions the transcripts stop in front of.

## The interpreter, transcribed

`03:6966` is the collision-table interpreter, and it is the busiest single
entry on the route: 33,513 entries with the registry deployed, 54,149 calls
when nothing is replaced at all. It advances one actor along a scripted
motion path -- a selector picks a table, two cursors walk the table and the
motion record it names, and the record steps the actor one unit at a time.

It had been replaced for a long time and the replacement was correct. What it
could not do was produce an instruction-boundary transcript on every path. It
emitted checkpoints for headerless one-step records with bounded axes and
returned an atomic body for everything else, and an atomic body is refused
whenever it would span a user event or a serviceable timer interrupt. That
refusal was costing 2,850 entries and 4.7M emulated master cycles, and it was
also what stopped the interpreter composing: both composition sites in the
tree gate on the child having checkpoints, and one of them additionally pins
the child to the single 165-cycle path.

That second gate, in `03:73A6`, has since been lifted, and what it turned up
is worth keeping. Removing it raised admission from 64 of 129 to 128 of 129
and moved both the state census and the write trace: the walk had no
checkpoint for the boundary the hit gate's `RTS` at `73FD` lands on, so an
interrupt arriving there was deferred to the boundary after the branch at
`73CA` and stacked `73CC` where ROM stacks `73CA`. The gate was not
protecting against the hazard its comment named; it was keeping the walk
inside the one child length whose calls never took an interrupt at that
boundary. With the checkpoint added, all three artifacts are identical to
assembly. **A guard that has never been crossed is not evidence that what
is behind it works.**

The reason this sat unstarted for several slices is that the replacement is
about 2,200 lines and rewriting it looked like the price of admission. It was
not. **The size of a replacement is not the size of the routine.** `03:6966`
is 0xD2 bytes and about a hundred instructions; the 2,200 lines are what it
cost to *predict* the routine's result from its entry state, which is what
the older method had to do. A transcript predicts nothing, so it needs the
instructions and nothing else. The walk is shorter than the code it fronts.

What the routine actually needs from the machine is small enough to state in
a sentence: work RAM, zero page, its own bank's ROM through two zero-page
pointers, and a stack page for one `PHP`, one `PHY` and a `JSR` into itself.
No device register, no call out of the bank, no bank change. The only control
flow that is not a straight line is its own -- a `BRA` back to the top of the
step when a record ends in a `$C0` terminator, and a `JSR`/`RTS` repeat loop
that executes ten times on the entire route.

## Composing the interpreter into the Sorcerer tick

Making `03:6966` a transcript did not compose it anywhere. It made composing
possible: both sites in the tree gate on the child carrying checkpoints, and
the consumer at `03:7F57` had been waiting on that and took its 749 calls for
free. The Sorcerer tick is the other site, and it is the one that had to be
written.

The tick calls the interpreter at `02:5890` and `02:58DA`, and a transcript
stops in front of a call -- which is why three of its five entry points exist
at all. Composing runs the call in line instead: push the return address, hand
the staged work RAM to the interpreter's own walk, splice the checkpoints and
writes it produced at the cycle the call landed on, and transcribe the `RTS`
that comes back. About a hundred and thirty lines, and none of it knows what
the interpreter means.

| `02:5886` native master cycles | 75,600 | 826,776 |
| Route-wide hook entries | 2,367,401 | 2,332,709 |

Gross was 1,538,439 and the shadowing bill 242,907 -- 15.8%, the second-lowest
of the eight measured: 15.6%, 18.1%, 21.0%, 19.7%, 17.8%, 20.4%, 19.7%, 15.8%.
Net `+1,295,532`. This is the first slice on which the route's *total* hook
entries fell, 2,367,401 to 2,332,709, because composing a call deletes the two
boundaries around it instead of adding one. That is not why the bill is low,
though, and it would be easy to claim otherwise: the other composing slice on
record, the scanner, billed 19.7% and came in slightly above what the rule
predicts. 15.8% sits inside the existing spread, next to a slice that composed
nothing.

Only two things stopped being emulated -- the two `JSR` instructions and the
interpreter's `RTS`, 731,907 cycles between them. The rest of the gross is
three replacements becoming one.

What is left at the composed-away entries is not decline. `02:5893` and
`02:58DD` are still entered 272 and 4,628 times and `03:6966` is still entered
746 times from this family, because an interrupt that lands on a checkpoint
inside the composed window is delivered there and the core runs the remainder
in assembly, which arrives at exactly those addresses. Of the 19,952 calls, at
most 79 could be a refusal rather than a resumption, and the trace cannot tell
those two apart because both leave the same footprint. They stay registered
for that reason.

Verification is the same two-run diff, widened by the stack depth the two
calls push to: 1,940,085 writes byte-identical hooked and unhooked in value,
address, order and master-cycle offset. `$21F7`-`$21FB` is in the range set
specifically for the pushes -- a `JSR` pushes both halves of the return address
before it pays for its own six cycles, and a push recorded at the end of the
instruction would leave every other write in the trace correct. The state
census equals the assembly baseline.

## Chunking the fade-to-black stepper

`00:EAA7` had been written off twice, in this document and in the inventory,
as 205,012 master cycles a call and so past the checkpoint cap however the
transcript was arranged. That sentence was true and the conclusion drawn
from it was not. The cap bounds a *transcript*, not a routine, and the VRAM
decoder had already shown what to do about it two slices earlier: chunk.

The stepper walks all thirty-two blocks of the shadow buffer in one call and
takes one step off every channel that is not already black, tallying the
colours that were into `$35`/`$36` so `00:EA77` knows when the fade is done.
512 colours a call is 5,300 work-RAM writes against a channel of 512.
`00:EAB0`, the head of the colour loop, becomes a second registered address;
a walk stops in front of the `JMP $EAB0` that closes the loop once a channel
is nearly full, and the core takes the jump and calls back. Eight calls, 101
transcripts, thirteen to a call.

| | Before | After |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| `00:EAA7` native master cycles | 0 | 137,829 |
| `00:EAB0` native master cycles | 0 | 1,514,691 |
| Palette region still emulated | 3,812,025 | 2,159,505 |
| Native share | 41.61% | 42.62% |

The routine costs 1,640,100 master cycles in assembly, measured on a run
with no registry at all and again on one with the whole registry deployed
— a leaf's cost does not depend on its neighbours. All but 1,284 of it is
now native; that remainder is the 93 jumps and 8 `RTS` instructions the core
still executes at the chunk boundaries and the return.

**The shadowing bill is 1,224 cycles, and that is not a general result.**
`00:E136` loses eight entries — the eight moments when a stepper chunk is
in flight as the VBlank body wants the hook — and nothing else on the route
moves at all. The rule of thumb, `gross / 3,000 × 575`, predicts 314,000. It
is wrong here by two orders of magnitude for a structural reason: the bill is
the cost of displacing *other* native routines, and the stepper runs during
screen fades, when the game is deliberately doing nothing else. A slice that
ports idle-time work bills almost nothing. This is the ninth measurement and
it does not belong in the same distribution as the other eight.

**It also found a flaw in this document's own headline.** The registry line
moved `+1,651,296`, which is more than the 1,640,100 the ported instructions
cost. The census adds a transcript's declared `body_master_cycles` when the
replacement is *offered* (`src/app/main.cpp`), before the core discovers that
an interrupt will truncate it, so a truncated transcript is credited with
work it did not do. Halving the chunk budget — 196 chunks instead of
101, same route, same byte-identical state census — *lowers* the total by
9,231, because shorter chunks lose less to each truncation. The excess over
the routine's true cost falls from 13,704 to 5,613. Every native share this
document has ever quoted is high by some small amount of this kind; the
effect is bounded by the transcript length and the interrupt rate, and on
this slice it is 0.8% of one routine. It has not been corrected anywhere,
and the 440-write budget was kept because it is the better engineering
choice — fewer hook entries — not the one that reports a larger number.

Verification is the two-run diff over the same three artifacts the palette
regression already asserted, and their hashes did not change. That is the
point rather than an oversight: the ranges already covered every byte the
stepper touches, so the artifact carries 42,770 writes from inside the
routine, which were byte-identical to assembly before it was ported and
still are — in value, address, order and master-cycle offset.

## What is left

Everything except the idle spin loop is now a porting target, because
the goal is a runtime with no emulator in it at all — video and audio
included. Earlier reports quoted a narrower "portable pool" that
excluded the graphics drivers; that distinction no longer means
anything and the figure has been dropped rather than carried forward
unverified.

Set the idle spin loop aside and 710,482,755 cycles are the machine
working; 677,903,067 of them, **95.41%**, now run natively.

The breakdown below is a self-consistent snapshot taken before the
interrupt dispatcher was split and the whole-palette arm with it, against
a slightly different measurement of the wait: 708,122,337 cycles outside
it, 670,996,452 native, 94.8%,
and a census remainder of 37,125,885. It is left at those numbers rather
than half-updated, because what it is for is the *shape* of the
remainder. Pricing the profile address by address divides it into two
kinds:

| Still emulated outside the spin loop | Master cycles |
| --- | ---: |
| Genuinely unported | 32,197,371 |
| Resumption after an interrupt inside a transcript | 8,581,961 |
| **Total** | **40,779,332** |

That total is 3,653,447 above what the census implies. The
overshoot is the method's own: each address is priced at one average
cost, so a branch that is taken more often under one run than the other
is charged the blend. The split is a ranking instrument, not an
accounting one, and the census is what settles the total. The
overshoot was 3,653,445 before the epilogue slice and 3,653,447 after,
which is the useful thing about it: it is a fixed error on addresses
the slice never touched, not a proportional one, so it does not grow
as the remainder shrinks — but it is now 9.8% of that remainder
rather than 5.9%, and every further slice makes it a larger share of
what is left to rank.

The last row is the hybrid's own overhead rather than work waiting to
be done: an interrupt landing inside a transcript resumes at a
checkpoint and interprets the rest of the body, which shows up as busy
addresses inside routines that are already fully ported. It disappears
with the emulator rather than before it.

The instrument is worth naming because it was not obvious it existed.
Run the deployed registry under `--instruction-profile`: the profile
does *not* count checkpoint-replayed instructions, so it drops from
8,977 addresses and 108.5M executions to 6,397 and 55.8M, and every
address that is not a registry entry point carries the cycles still
being emulated at it.

Three corrections make it usable, and all three were found by trusting
the profile too far first.

A native body absorbs its cycles into the last instruction sampled before
it, because the profiler charges each sample the span to the next one and
a replacement produces no samples. That is normally the entry point, but
any instruction that precedes a native call is inflated the same way --
the `RTI` at `00:FC8D` returns *into* a suspended transcript and carried
99,636,684 cycles of other routines' work. The fix is not a blacklist:
price each address at its executions times its cost from the
*assembly-only* profile, where nothing is replaced and so nothing can be
absorbed. Every leak goes at once.

Second, a busy address is not evidence of unported code. Compare the
same address's executions in both runs: `03:6967` runs 54,149 times
under assembly and 119 times under the registry, so it is 99.8% ported
and its 1.2M is resumption after an interrupt. `00:E1A0` ran 17,995
times in both, which is what unported actually looks like. **That ratio
is the ranking instrument, not the cycle count.** It retired three
entries from this file's own next-target list: `08:40DE`-`41AB` with
`08:4085`-`4094`, recorded as 2.8M, is four `JSR`s and an `RTS` around a
routine that was already native.

Third, a registry entry point is sampled but never executed. The
instruction hook runs before the replacement hook, so every native call
leaves one sample on its entry address for an instruction the core then
skips. Priced from assembly that phantom is 37,655,643 route-wide --
36% of everything a straight read of the profile calls "still
emulated", and enough on its own to make the previous estimate of the
sound driver's blocked arrivals too large by half. Dropping entry rows
is what makes the books close: native plus spin loop plus what is left
comes back to the elapsed total within 9.8%.

That instrument found the epilogue slice, which has now been taken, and
the row it used to occupy is gone. The history is worth keeping because
it is the clearest case the project has of an estimate being wrong in
both directions at once. Until replacements could nest, the whole sound
driver read as one undifferentiated 110,519,836-cycle block recorded
here as "entries the no-nesting rule blocks": 37.7M of that was phantom
entry-point samples that were never executed at all, and hidden inside
the rest was a real 26M target nobody could see. Nesting recovered
85,778,658 and exposed the epilogue; the epilogue slice then took
25,180,701 of what it exposed.

What is left of the driver is 6,880,569 cycles in three pieces, and
each is left deliberately. The `RTI`s at `00:FC8D` and `00:FD81` are
5,349,879: returning from an interrupt is the frame boundary's work,
not a transcript's. The `SEI`s at `00:FC81` and `00:FD75` are
1,526,046, and are the finding of that slice — see the epilogue
section above for why a checkpoint cannot describe them. The two
`JMP $FC03` chain exits are 4,644.

The rest is spread thin: 32,197,371 cycles, 2.49% of the machine, of
which the driver's deliberate 6.9M is the largest single item and
nothing else is above 1.6M. The largest are `08:41AB` (1.56M),
`01:C700`-`C77F` (1.22M), `08:4080`-`40FF` (1.20M), `01:DC80`-`DCFF`
(950K), `00:E200`-`E27F` (894K), `03:6000`-`607F` (857K),
`01:C100`-`C17F` (839K) and `00:E000`-`E07F` (775K).

**Porting by hotspot is finished now, and this time the claim is
instrumented.** It was withdrawn once, after being made while the
driver's epilogue was still hidden behind an over-attributed nesting
bill, so it is worth saying exactly what is different. Every block
above 1.6M has been taken; the largest single thing still emulated is
now a deliberate refusal rather than an unexplored target; and the
ranking method's own error, 3,653,447 cycles, is a tenth of the
remainder it is ranking, which is the point where the instrument stops
being able to find the next slice for you. Further progress comes from
the phases in
[EMULATOR_ELIMINATION.md](EMULATOR_ELIMINATION.md) — device state and
time — not from more transcripts.

The pad reader used to head this list at 6.9M, described here as out of
reach because a callback cannot read a device register. That was true of
the callback and false of the routine. The interrupt dispatcher was then
listed beside it for the same reason, and that was false of the routine
too: only its `LDA $0000` is unportable, and only because the read is the
interrupt acknowledge rather than because the value is unknowable. Both
sides of it are native; see the VDC interrupt handler section above.
What both cases have in common is that the address someone wrote down as
blocked was a routine, and the thing that was actually blocked was one
instruction inside it.

Note that this percentage tracks only one of the three axes that
removing the emulator needs: which code executes natively. It does not
track device state or time. A native routine today still writes into
Beetle's VDC and runs on Beetle's clock, so the figure will
plateau well short of "the emulator is removable". See
[EMULATOR_ELIMINATION.md](EMULATOR_ELIMINATION.md) for the phases the
percentage does not cover.

The sound generator is the one device that has come off that list,
and it is a clean demonstration of what the percentage misses. The
PSG at `$0800`-`$0809` is now `src/audio/pce_psg.cpp`, a native
device model rather than Beetle's; the writes the driver emits
reach it instead. That moved the native share by zero cycles,
because a sound chip executes no instructions, and it is still
real progress toward deleting the emulator.

It also shows what a route census can and cannot prove. The model
counts its own paths, and `--report-psg-coverage` reports them:
over the census route its LFO runs zero times, because every
store in the ROM to the byte the sequencer loads the LFO control
register from writes a zero. Paths the game does not reach are
proved instead against the emulator's own device, in
`tests/psg_device_tests.cpp`.

Five whole loops are now native instruction for instruction: the main
actor loop (`01:C510`, `01:C51E`, `01:C520`, `01:C523`, `01:C52C`,
`01:C606`, `01:C654`), the collision sweep (`01:CB48`, `01:CB61`,
`01:CB6B`), the camera-visibility sweep (`00:F4F2`, `00:F4FC`, `00:F523`,
`00:F545`, `00:F54E`), the SAT submission (`08:4036`, `08:4063`,
`08:4075`, `08:4088`, `08:4097`, `08:40C3`, `08:40DF`), and the timer
sound driver (`00:FC00`, `00:FC82`, `00:FD76`).

Cycle share is concentrated, and the epilogue slice concentrated it
further. The timer sound driver `00:FC00` is now the single largest
native address at 12.25% of all master cycles — it passed the collision
sweep `01:CB48` at 7.96% and the sprite emitter `08:40DF` at 7.59% by
taking its own epilogue — and the top five together are 31.97% of the
machine, well over half of the 52.21% that runs natively. Most installed
routines are never entered on this route, and many that are entered never
accept a call.
